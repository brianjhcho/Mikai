## 2. Tier 2 diagnosis — why user-defined goals go invisible

**Culprit (a) — the source — is real but subtler than stated.** Current *does* contain three live personal goals (proposal/Germaine, monstera/moss pole, rib flare). What it entirely lacks is items 2, 6, 7, 10: ocean farming, tech job/company, parents, settling a family. Why? **Circularity.** USER_MODEL.md is compiled by the same dream pass from the same corpus. Goals under-represented in the corpus can never appear in a corpus-derived goal model, so the goal term inherits exactly the blindspots it was designed to correct. Four of six Current bullets are MIKAI-build state — the corpus bias reproduced in miniature.

**Culprit (b) — the matching — is the binding failure even where the source is right.** Name-token intersection produced goal_overlap values of 0–3 across 1,829 candidates, against a base term spanning −2.5 to 7.5 and spread spanning 1–7. The term is structurally incapable of moving a rank even when it fires (max observed: 3, on `monstera-moss-pole`). And it can't fire on semantic matches: "finding a job in tech" shares zero ≥4-char tokens with "Vancouver Startup Week." Keyword overlap is the wrong instrument for goal semantics; the brief's own design (E2 + embedding max-sim) knew this and the MVP substituted a weaker mechanism.

**The deeper question: separate score. Yes — goals do not belong inside S.** Three arguments. First, *declared goals are queries, not corpus statistics.* S measures evidence in the record; goal alignment G measures what the user asserts matters. Summing them into one scalar means declaring 20 goals tomorrow perturbs every candidate's S and reshuffles the entire ledger — churn with no new evidence, and it drowns whichever signal has smaller dynamic range (today: G, always). Second, *re-ranking should be instant and cheap*: G changes when goals change, S changes when the corpus changes; separate axes mean editing a goals file re-sorts without recomputing corpus statistics. Third — the decisive one — **the disagreement between the axes is the product.** High-G/low-S = a declared goal going cold ("parents": 185 mentions, no thread) — that is Sumimasen's stall signal. High-S/low-G = an unacknowledged preoccupation. Summing destroys the gap; keeping two columns *is* the noonchi feature. Promote by union of top-K on each axis, and report the gap explicitly.

## 3. Tier 3 diagnosis — inference-based salience, is it real?

Yes, with a hard boundary. The closest prior art, in ascending relevance: **LDA** (Blei, Ng & Jordan 2003) — co-occurrence topics, but unlabeled and famously uninterpretable at personal-corpus scale; **BERTopic** (Grootendorst 2022) — embedding clusters + c-TF-IDF labels, deterministic and cheap, labels still crude; **Anthropic's Clio** (2024) — LLM-summarize each unit, embed, cluster, LLM-label the cluster; validated in production at massive scale, and its key lesson is that the LLM labels *clusters of evidence*, never free-generates themes; and **Generative Agents' reflection mechanism** (Park et al. 2023) — periodically ask "what high-level insights can you infer from these N memories?", store inferences as first-class memory objects *citing the evidence memories*. That last one is almost literally item 1's requirement. (I don't have a confident memory of a Meta "LookupTables" paper and won't cite it.)

Reliability splits on method. **Cluster-then-label over retrieved evidence is reliable enough to ship**: given 40 sections about productivity gaps, wealth creation, and societal evolution, a current LLM will produce "political economy / how economic systems distribute power" with high consistency — that's a bounded summarization task. **Free-form inference over the whole corpus is not**: it invites the failure mode where a wrong latent theme becomes a "concept" — and this failure is qualitatively worse than a bad ranking, because it is a *claim about the person* ("you care about X") that the user doesn't recognize. For Brian specifically this violates the provenance-over-plausibility rule already in USER_MODEL's Durable section (the invented-partner incident). One confabulated identity-level theme poisons trust in the whole ledger, and the brief's confabulation-loop warning applies doubly: a wrong theme, once a page, gets re-ingested as fact.

**Safe-to-ship version**: embedding-cluster the sections that match *no* existing candidate (the residue the string layer missed), have the LLM label only clusters with ≥N sections, and every inferred concept lands in a quarantined "Inferred (unconfirmed)" ledger section citing its source section IDs — never auto-promoted, user confirms with one keystroke. That's the `connect` quarantine design applied to themes. **Impossible version**: unsupervised latent themes written directly into top ranks and concept pages. The difference is not model quality; it's whether the human is the promotion gate.

## 4. What the recall gap actually tells us about the architecture

1/10 is not a tuning problem; it is a **construct-validity failure compounded by a candidate-coverage ceiling**. Take them in order.

**The ceiling first, because it binds.** Six of ten gold concepts (9, 7, 1, 6, and the coherent forms of 3 and 10) had zero or fragment-only candidates. Recall@10 was bounded at ~0.4 *before a single score was computed*. Whatever else changes, extraction — lowercase phrases, alias folding, theme residue — is the first-order fix. Weight-tuning on the current candidate pool cannot exceed the ceiling.

**Then the construct.** S measures "what recurs in this corpus." Brian's free-list measures "what matters to me." These correlate only when the corpus is a fair sample of the life — and this corpus is radically not: MIKAI ingests its own build sessions, so it's a self-referential record where the tool stack dominates every term simultaneously (frequency, recency, *and* spread). `claude`, `wiki`, `graphiti`, `gmail` aren't formula errors; they are correct answers to the wrong question.

**Should one formula answer both questions? No.** Keep the formula answering "what recurs" — it's deterministic, auditable, reproducible, and it is the only layer that can run nightly at zero LLM cost. Add "what matters" as a **second stage over the recurrence substrate**, with two components the original brief already designed and the MVP cut: G (goal alignment, E2 — declared + inferred goals, embedding-matched) and I(c) (LLM importance, E3 — the brief's own words: "the one failure the formula provably cannot fix"). The MVP shipped only the recurrence half; 1/10 recall is the *predicted* outcome of that cut, not a surprise. Architecture: formula = candidate generator + evidence prior over 1,800 candidates; the mattering stage = re-ranker over the top ~200, cheap enough to be LLM-touched nightly. And per §2, don't collapse the stages into one scalar — the S/G disagreement is the surfacing signal L4 consumes.
