## Overview
[[entities/ray-dalio]]'s term for the 50–75 year arc during which debt accumulates faster than income until the burden becomes unsustainable — a macroeconomic framework describing how credit-driven growth cycles eventually exhaust themselves, with an inflationary expansion phase fueled by rising debt and easy credit giving way to a deflationary contraction as debt burdens become unsustainable and credit contracts. It is distinguished from the short-term debt cycle (5–8 years, driven by central bank policy) by its span — longer than an individual career's memory, which is why most participants fail to see it coming.

## Notes

**Mechanism.** The long-term cycle is generated by the short-term cycle's political dynamics. Central banks cut interest rates to escape each short-term downturn, reducing debt service and enabling continued borrowing. The downside of each short cycle is therefore smoothed at the cost of a higher debt level at the next cycle's floor. Compounded across decades, debt-to-income ratios rise secularly until debt service consumes so large a share of income — and rates are so low there is no further room to cut — that a major deleveraging becomes unavoidable.

**The basic arc.**
1. Debt and spending rise faster than income; asset prices inflate.
2. Central banks tighten to control inflation (rate hikes).
3. Credit contracts; spending falls; deflationary pressure builds.
4. Deleveraging follows — painful but necessary to reset the cycle.

**At the limit.** When the long-term cycle peaks, exactly four policy levers remain:
1. **Austerity and default** (deflationary)
2. **Debt monetization / printing** (inflationary)
3. **Wealth transfers** (redistributive)

*(Tension: the canonical text says "exactly four policy levers" but enumerates only three. Retained as written; the count and the list disagree in the source.)*

The outcome depends on how these are mixed. A [[concepts/beautiful-deleveraging]] balances the levers so nominal growth exceeds nominal interest rates — reducing the debt-to-income ratio without triggering deflationary depression or hyperinflation.

**Nesting.** The long-term debt cycle nests inside the [[concepts/reserve-currency-cycle]]: reserve status grants cheap borrowing that seeds the long-term cycle's accumulation phase; the cycle's end often coincides with the erosion of reserve status.

**Empirical basis.** Dalio studied 48 historical debt cycles and compressed them into two archetypal templates — deflationary (domestic-currency debt) and inflationary (foreign-currency debt). The [[concepts/denomination-ownership-test]] determines which template applies to any given crisis.

**Intellectual ancestry.** The long-term debt cycle operationalizes [[entities/hyman-minsky]]'s financial instability hypothesis across the full historical record. Where Minsky identifies the endogenous hedge→speculative→Ponzi progression, Dalio maps the same dynamic across centuries and derives quantifiable thresholds. See [[concepts/financial-instability-hypothesis]].
